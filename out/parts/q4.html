EIA basics (L11–L13)

  • EIA (Sadler 1996): identifying, predicting, evaluating, mitigating biophysical, social and other effects prior to major decisions. Middle path (Madhyam-Pratipada); balances development vs environment.
  • Core values: Integrity, Utility, Sustainability. Purpose: facilitate decision-making; aid sustainable development; instrument for sustainable development.
  • Types: Rapid (1 season) / Comprehensive (4 seasons); Regional, Sectoral, Strategic (SEA = EIA of PPP), Project-level, LCA. EIA end-of-cycle, reactive vs SEA early, proactive.
  • Impact classes: beneficial/detrimental; reversible/irreversible; repairable/irreparable; short/long term; local/regional/national.
  • Stages: Project proposal → Screening → Scoping → Baseline → Impact prediction/assessment → Mitigation → EIS → Review (public consultation) → Licensing → Follow-up/monitoring.
  • Screening: is EIA needed? (threshold & impact criteria; inclusion/exclusion lists; categories A/B/C). Scoping: boundaries → TOR. Baseline: current status. World Bank A/B/C; C e.g. PMGSY village roads.
  • Mitigation: prevent / limit severity & duration / compensate. Hierarchy Avoid → Mitigate → Restore → Offset. IMP elements: mitigation measures, monitoring, capacity development, implementation schedule & cost, integration. EIS = final stage; review by MoEF (India); licensing = outcome of review; follow-up checks accuracy of predictions.
