Lecture 7

  • L7·s6 Transportation-induced health impacts (Khreis 2017): Urban transport policy and planning → Built environment ↔ Transport provision and mode choice → outcomes list: Motor vehicle crashes · Air pollution · Noise · Heat islands · Lack of green space and biodiversity loss · Physical inactivity · Social exclusion · Community severance · Climate change → Premature mortality and Morbidity.
  • L7·s7 Planning decisions chain (Litman 2013): Planning decision (infrastructure investment, parking requirements, fees and taxes, traffic management) → Land use patterns (development location, density and mix, parking supply and price, building orientation) → Travel behavior (amount of walking, cycling, public transit, vehicle ownership, automobile travel) → Economic, social and environmental impacts (consumer costs, public service costs, crashes, pollution emissions, physical fitness).
  • L7·s14 Vehicle emissions and efficiency (EEA 2016): fossil-fuel road transport = most significant source of transport-related air pollution. Fuel energy 100% → engine losses 70% (thermal 60%, combustion, pumping, friction); parasitic losses 5%; drivetrain losses 5%; power to wheels 20% (rolling resistance 5%, wind resistance 10%, braking 5%). Emission sources: refueling losses (HC, VOC); evaporating emissions (HC, VOC); exhaust (CO2, CO, HC, NOx, PM); abrasion of tyres, brakes and clutch (PM); road suspension wear (PM); re-suspension of road dust (PM).
  • L7·s30 Deaths due to air pollution, 2019 (IHME): China 1,848,000; India 1,667,000; Pakistan 235,700; Nigeria 197,600; Indonesia 186,300; Bangladesh 173,500; Egypt 91,700; Russia 77,500; Ethiopia 77,000; Philippines 74,800; Myanmar 74,500; Vietnam 71,700; Brazil 60,900; United States 60,200; Japan 42,600; Poland 31,100; Germany 29,300; Italy 27,900; UK 15,000; France 14,300; Spain 11,800.

  • L7·s32 DPSEEA framework (WHO): Driving force – urbanization, migration, transport policy (action: economic, transport policies). Pressure – rise in number of vehicles; increase commuting by vehicles; emission of pollutants, noise (action: emission control). State – congested roads, hazardous driving situation; decrease physical activity; increase lead & toxic gases, noise (action: environmental improvement). Exposure – risk for road traffic accidents; sedentary lifestyle, less energy expenditure; exposure to pollutants, noise (action: awareness, education). Effect – road traffic injuries, road rage; obesity, diabetes, CVD; respiratory disease, noise-induced health problems (action: treatment).

Lecture 9

  • L9·s5 Photo: interrupted subsurface water flow due to road openings (World Bank 2019).
  • L9·s30 Sources of transportation air pollution (EPA): On-road: light-duty vehicles, heavy-duty vehicles, motorcycles. Nonroad: aircraft, nonroad diesel equipment, marine engines/ocean vessels, locomotives, lawn & garden, recreation vehicles. Smog and soot → health and welfare impacts; CO2 and other GHGs → climate change. Solutions (emission reductions → cleaner air & better health): catalytic converters (with unleaded gasoline and low sulfur levels reduce hydrocarbon & nitrogen oxide emissions); fuel standards (reduce exposure to pollutants like lead and benzene; renewable fuels reduce CO2); engine technologies (computer controls, variable valve timing, multi-valve engines, turbo charging, gasoline direct injection); transmission technologies (7+ speeds, dual clutch transmissions, continuously variable transmissions); diesel filters (reduce particulate matter from on-road and off-road diesel engines); alternative vehicle technologies (plug-in electric vehicles & fuel cells = zero tailpipe emissions); better transportation planning (for passengers & freight reduces emissions & fuel use).

  • L10·s23 Decibel formula: Lp = ln(p/p0) Np = 2 log10(p/p0) B = 20 log10(p/p0) dB (as in notes).
  • L10·s26 Leq formula: Leq = 10 log [ (1/(t2−t1)) ∫t1t2 (pA²/p0²) dt ]; Leq = equivalent continuous sound pressure level in dB; p0 = reference pressure (typically 20 µPa); pA = acquired sound pressure; t1 = start time; t2 = end time. The equation divides the integrated, normalized sound pressure by the duration of interest; result in decibels.

  • L10·s46 Insertion loss (Kurze & Anderson): IL = 5 dB + 20 log [ √(2πN) / tanh√(2πN) ] dB for N up to 12.5; IL = 20 dB for N > 12.5. N = (a + b − l) f / C0 (Fresnel number).
