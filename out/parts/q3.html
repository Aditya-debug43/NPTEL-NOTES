Lecture 17 — EIA Case Study-I: Mumbai–Ahmedabad High Speed Rail (MAHSR) (39 slides)

Project L17·s3–11

  • First proposed in Indian Railways' "VISION 2020" (Ministry of Railways); high speed corridors allow up to 350 kmph; to use Shinkansen trains from Japan; connects Mumbai–Ahmedabad; completion expected 2028.
  • Shinkansen (s4): popularly "Bullet Train"; famous for zero casualty from train accident in 50 years with 10 billion passengers; literal meaning "New trunk/main line".
  • EIA report (s5): proponent = National High Speed Rail Corporation Limited (NHSRCL). Prepared by: GPS Technologies Pvt. Ltd – S-EIA report, primary/secondary environmental data; NHSRCL / RITES – land acquisition plan; ARCADIS – Resettlement and Rehabilitation Action Plan. (RITES = Rail India Technical and Economic Services.)
  • NHSRCL (s6–7): incorporated 12 February 2016 under Companies Act 2013; to finance, construct, maintain, manage HSR corridor; modelled as Special Purpose Vehicle (SPV) in joint sector; equity by Central Government (Ministry of Railways) and governments of Gujarat and Maharashtra. SPV = joint venture/legal entity for a specific objective; collaboration of multiple parties; reduces risk for any single entity.
  • Location (s8): 508 km; 12 stations (Mumbai (BKC), Thane, Virar, Boisar, Vapi, Billimora, Surat, Bharuch, Vadodara, Anand, Ahmedabad, Sabarmati); Gujarat 1,100 hectares/195 villages; Maharashtra 350 hectares/104 villages.
  • Benefits (s9): travel time 6.5 hrs → 2.5 hrs; connectivity boost to Palghar, Valsad, Vapi, Bharuch; technological enhancement; jobs – construction ~20,000, operations & maintenance 4,000, 16,000 indirect; decongestion of rail, road, air; GHG reduction; state-of-the-art manufacturing and software ecosystem.

Lecture 18 — EIA Case Study-II: Hunan Xiangjiang Inland Waterway, China (42 slides)

Project L18·s3–14

  • Project: construction of a multi-purpose river barrage, improve navigational draft along a 50 km reach of Xiangjiang River, generate hydropower. Lender: Asian Development Bank (ADB). EIA report by Hunan Xiangjiang Navigation Construction & Development Co., Ltd. (2011).
  • ADB (s4): envisions prosperous, inclusive, resilient, sustainable Asia and Pacific; loans, technical assistance, grants, equity investments; 68 member countries including India.
  • Location (s5): middle-to-lower reaches of Xiangjiang River, tributary of the Yangtze; Tugutang Complex ~38 km upstream from Hengyang and ~50 km downstream from Jinweizhou Complex.
  • Main components (s6): Tugutang barrage to raise water levels and flood dangerous shoals along 50 km; hydropower station 90 MW, normal annual energy 358.2 MkWh; two new river wharves; works in inundated areas incl. resettlement of farmers.
  • Need (s7–8): hilly terrain/narrow valley constrain road and rail; poor navigation; industrial output along river = 60% of province; freight predicted 0.20 and 0.29 billion tons by 2020 and 2030 from 20 million tons in 2010; energy consumption of unit transport ≈ 65% of railways and 15% of highway; CO2 emissions from water transport < half of rail and 22% of highway.
  • ADB EIA (s10): Safeguard Policy Statement (2009), like World Bank; project screened and categorized Category A (potential significant adverse impacts).
  • Chinese laws (s11): 1989 Environment protection law; 1997 Prevention & control of environmental noise pollution; 1998 Protection of wildlife; 1998 Flood control law; 2001 Evaluation of environmental effects; 2002 Water law; 2004 Fishery law; 2005 Prevention & control of pollution by solid waste; 2008 Prevention & control of water pollution; 2009 Prevention & control of atmospheric pollution.
  • EIA steps in China (s12): environmental baseline data; project alternatives; recommendations for minimizing/mitigating/eliminating impacts; analysis of extent and distribution of adverse impacts and consequences; opinions of affected persons; environmental monitoring program.
  • No-project scenario (s13): river not navigable during dry season between lower and upper sections; cargo loaded off ships onto road/rail between Jinweizhou and Hengyang (88 km). IWT advantages (s14): geography suitable; lower operating cost per ton-km than road and rail; no road congestion/rail capacity issues; relatively safe and secure.

Lecture 19 — EIA Case Study-III: NH-707 hilly road, Himachal Pradesh (38 slides)

Project L19·s3–14

  • Gumma to Fediz (NH-707); MoRTH development of single/intermediate lane NHs; National Highways Interconnectivity Project (NHIIP) aided by the World Bank; EIA report by "Consulting Engineers Associates, Panchkula".
  • Benefits (s4): reduction of Vehicle Operating Cost (VOC) (less roughness, improved alignment); improved financial sustainability for road development and maintenance; increased vehicle speeds; reduced passenger time costs.
  • EIA steps (s5): study of background info; screening report; Survey Part-1 (baseline data collection); Survey Part-2 (socio-economic); analysis of practical impacts; meeting with stakeholders; mitigation measures adoption; Environment Management Plan.
  • Physical features (s6–8): hilly tract with undulating terrain; elevation 2500 m to 3500 m above MSL; deep valleys, river terraces, steep escarpments, streams. Geology: alluvial deposits, sandstone, clay top zone, variable rocks. Seismology: moderately earthquake prone. Landslides: soil stability major issue; landslips, mud flows, flash floods; rises in rainy season. 8 major landslide points (chainage km): 95.530–95.580; 95.400–95.430; 96.520–96.570; 98.800–98.850; 101.170–101.270; 101.370–101.470; 101.690–101.730; 102.630–102.690.
  • Policies (s9): National Forest Policy 1988; National Water Policy 2012; National Environment Policy 2006; National Conservation Strategy 1992; National Policy on Resettlement and Rehabilitation for Project Affected Families 2007. Laws (s10): EP Act 1986; Forest (Conservation) Act 1980 (amended 2003 & 2004); Wildlife (Protection) Act (amended 2002); Air Act 1972 [as written]; Water Act 1974; EIA Notification 2006 (& amendments).
  • Clearances (s11–12): GOI: widening of existing State Highway – does not fall under any EC category per EIA 2006. World Bank: classified Category B; cumulative impacts considered. State Ground Water Board: clearance for ground water extraction. State pollution control board: consent for establishment and operation of construction plants. Forest clearance from Forest dept for tree cutting.
  • With vs without project (s13–14): without – poor connectivity/pavement, high travel time, pollution, restricted access to markets, medical, education; no impact on ecology/social. With – improved connectivity, reduced travel time and pollution, increased trade; felling of 186 trees (compensatory afforestation); 03 structures (encroachments) affected, no common property resources affected.

Lecture 20 — EIA Case Study-IV: Guwahati airport integrated terminal (36 slides)

Project L20·s3–13

  • New Integrated Terminal Building at Lokpriya Gopinath Bordoloi International Airport (LGBI) / Guwahati International Airport; proponent Airport Authority of India (AAI); EIA by "ABC Techno Labs India Private Limited".
  • AAI (s4): constituted by an Act of Parliament in 1995; creates, upgrades, maintains, manages civil aviation infrastructure; manages 137 airports; air navigation services over 2.8 million square nautical miles of airspace.
  • Guwahati airport (s5–7): major airport in North-East India, Assam; inter-region hub; suits medium aircraft (B-767/A-310); before project: 75 flight movements/day, 525/week; saturated; land 528.225 acres. No alternative site considered – existing terminal saturated; site within airport (s8).
  • Planned project (s9): centrally air-conditioned modular terminal with multilevel car parking; designed for 2900 domestic and 200 international passengers at a time; Level of Service "B" per IATA initially, "C" at saturation. LOS (s10): A Excellent comfort; B High; C Good; D Adequate; E Inadequate; F Unacceptable — depends on space per passenger and maximum waiting time; IATA changes standards from time to time.
  • Benefits (s11): better passenger facilities; tourism, trade, commerce; revenue; employment; business and industrial opportunities.
  • EC requirement (s12): per EIA 2006 all projects incl. airstrips for commercial use need EC from MoEF&CC; Category A; air strips without bunkering/refueling facility and/or Air Traffic Control are exempted. ICAO norms (s13): reducing noise; land use planning; reducing emissions at source; operational mechanisms; market-based options (emissions permit trading, charges, voluntary agreements).
  • Community consultation (s14): group discussions; issues: employment, information flow, health & safety, social welfare; people expect preference for locals in construction jobs.
  • Impact rating matrix (s15): Negligible – change comparable to natural variation; Minor – detectable but not significant; Moderate – insignificant; amenable to mitigation; should be mitigated where practicable; Major – significant; amenable to mitigation; must be mitigated.
