L32 — Sustainable Transportation Planning & Approaches-III: Management Strategies

Transportation System Management (TSM) s3

  • Solution to peak-hour congestion, delay, accidents in cities. Widening roads, elevated flyovers, bypasses, urban expressways may prove costly.
  • TSM: a set of measures adopted to ensure the most productive and cost-effective use of existing transportation services and modes.
  • Strategies covered (s2): prohibited turning; one-way streets; car pooling; staggering office hours; chartered buses; peripheral parking; internal CBD shuttle; transport rationing; congestion pricing; parking restrictions; day-time truck restriction; real-time travel information; contraflow lanes; exclusive bus lanes.

Strategies

StrategyKey facts from slides
Turning restrictions s4–7Can be established only if the street is capable of alternate routing. Prohibited right/left/U turns increase saturation flow and capacity at an intersection; cannot solve congestion wholly, but assists other techniques. Replaced by: T-turn = diversion of right-turning traffic to an alternative intersection capable of more traffic; G-turn = diversion of right-turning traffic to the left before the junction; Q-turn = diversion of right-turning traffic beyond the junction. Example: Menlo Park (Willows neighborhood, California) – table: total traffic on Durham Street (at Willow Rd) 300 (2017) → 78 (2019), −74%; right-turn Durham St→Willow Rd 218 → 17 (−92%, prohibited in 2019 data); Willow Road (at Durham) total 1,164 → 1,611 (+38%); right-turn Willow Rd→Durham St 5 → 4 (−20%).
One-way streets s8–9Movement permitted in only one direction; improves flow, increases capacity, reduces delays; least expensive strategy; beneficial when combined with banned turns, signals etc. Example: Karol Bagh, New Delhi (one-way circulation plan).
Car pooling s10–13Travellers share ride from/to common origin/destination. Types: casual/informal; real-time (app-based); van pooling: 7 to 15 passengers sharing cost of a van as a daily mode. Reduces private vehicles, congestion, fuel cost. Social benefits: reduced vehicle miles travelled; reduced fuel consumption & GHG; reduced air-pollution impacts; cost savings for public agencies & employers. U.S.: car pooling is the 2nd most common travel mode to work after driving alone; its mode share has decreased (chart: carpool 19.7% in 1980 → 9.0% at last plotted point; drive alone 64.4% → 76.3%) [read from graph]. Dale City example: left lanes open to all traffic; right lanes only for buses and cars with minimum three riders.
Staggering office & school hours s14–16Employees choose office hours, core time fixed (ex. 10 A.M–7 P.M or 7 A.M–4 P.M); reduces peak-hour traffic & improves public transport conditions; same for school hours. Sample chart: flexible morning/evening hours keeping core time and total weekly hours same. Linköping (Sweden) cost-benefit of (a) one-hour-later school day vs (b) half-hour staggered start (million SEK/yr): later school day – benefit 8 / cost 8.5; staggered – benefit 8 / cost 11.3 (benefit items: buses not needed 8; costs: pupils, teachers, principal). Staggered scenario: 1/3 classes start half hour earlier, 1/3 half hour later, 1/3 same time as today. 1 SEK = 8.61 INR.
Peripheral parking (park & ride) s17–18Public parks private vehicle at city outskirts and uses public transport to city centre; integrate with car pooling, BRTS, metro; reduces fuel cost, avoids congestion tiredness. Example: Amsterdam.
Chartered (institutional) buses s19Connect urban centres; trips from city centre to business parks, schools & colleges, government offices; reduce private vehicle inflow, congestion, fuel use; good connectivity to city centre needed.
Internal shuttle in CBD s20–21Uninterrupted shuttle bus services on all routes within city; better connectivity; integration with park & ride increases demand; mobile apps & real-time information increase passenger interest. Example: National University of Singapore (NUS) institutional & shuttle buses.
Congestion pricing s22–25Demand-based; encourages private vehicles to shift away from congested routes in peak hours; private vehicles using route in peak hours fined heavily. Advantages: reduces peak congestion; efficient use of system (alternate routes/public modes); smooth flow saves time & delay; revenue generation; fewer accidents; reduced emissions & idling. Example 1 – London: cordon pricing, charge for private vehicles entering Central London from early 2003; successful despite opposition. Example 2 – Singapore: limiting car purchases, high licence cost, electronic tolls on highways, cordon pricing in downtown (CBD pricing zone).
Parking restrictions s26–28In city centre indirectly reduce vehicle flow. Benefits: increased automobile occupancy; decrease in person trips; faster travel & less delay; more public transport use; less air pollution; low ambient noise. London = Controlled Parking Zone (CPZ): all parking controlled by hours, only in designated bays; hours Mon–Fri 7am–7pm; Saturday 7am–11am. Chart: monthly CBD parking rates 2011 – highest London ≈ $1,083.59, lowest Boston ≈ $438 (others e.g. Zurich $822.15, Hong Kong $744.72, Tokyo $744.00) [read from chart].
Transport rationing s29–33Restricts number of vehicles or their movement; examples: no-drive day, driving restriction, odd-even schemes. Odd-even (Delhi): rationing mechanism; in theory controls pollution by reducing traffic & congestion by restricting on-road vehicles per day; applied when ambient air quality deteriorated much. Limitations: followed in Paris, Rome, Bogota, Mexico City, Beijing; mostly failed after initial temporary success; people buy a cheap secondary car; low public transport capacity/poor infrastructure causes distress. Only-pedestrian on weekends: for very high footfall places; Connaught Place (more than a million footfall daily); NDMC planned to restrict vehicles Saturdays & Sundays in inner circle → trader protest → rolled back. No-Car Sundays, Jakarta (capital of Indonesia): two Sundays a month; "Car-Free Sunday"; concept gaining traction incl. India & Singapore.
Day-time truck restriction s34–35Time restriction on heavy vehicles on certain roads in peak hours; separating heavy vehicles gives: less delay, enhanced safety, reduced congestion, noise & air pollution. Delhi–Gurugram Expressway: 14 km stretch, no truck entry 8–10 A.M and 5–10 P.M.
Real-time travel information s36–38Infrastructure: central data repositories fed by developers/travelers/media-3rd parties; data sources shown: traffic, events, parking, weather, signals, CAD, transit, infrastructure. Benefits: increase system efficiency & capacity; enhance mobility; improve safety; reduce energy consumption & environmental costs; increase economic productivity.
Contraflow lanes s39–43Lane made exclusively for peak-hour traffic in one direction; better connectivity, less congestion/travel time. Ex.: single lane of two-way road towards business park is contraflow in morning peak, opposite direction in evening peak. Usually for public transport, emergency vehicles, bicyclists. Examples: contraflow bus lane; contraflow bicycle lane on one-way street; Dublin (allowing entry in opposite direction only for cyclists).
Exclusive bus lanes s44–46Reserve a lane exclusively for buses; possible only where adequate carriageway width (minimum 3 lanes); adjacent to curb to board & embark; exclusive bus/cycle lanes may also be contraflow lanes. Example: Ahmedabad.

Taming the complexity / Conclusions s47–48

  • Sustainable transportation possible only with integrated effort of individuals, families, organizations, governments at local, regional, state, national levels.
  • TSM ensures productive & cost-effective solution to peak congestion, delay, accidents; right strategy or combination gives good results; reduces private vehicles, congestion, delays, fuel cost, emissions.
Important Distinctions to Remember (L32)
  • T-turn (alternative intersection) vs G-turn (left before junction) vs Q-turn (beyond junction).
  • Contraflow lane (peak-hour, opposite direction) vs exclusive bus lane (needs minimum 3 lanes, next to curb); both may be contraflow.
  • Car pooling types: casual, real-time (app), van pooling (7–15). Park & ride = outskirts parking + public transport; CBD shuttle = uninterrupted shuttle within the city.
  • Congestion pricing (fine for peak-hour use; London 2003 cordon; Singapore) vs parking restrictions (indirect reduction) vs rationing (odd-even; fails with second cheap car).
