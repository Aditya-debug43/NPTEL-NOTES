L39 — Circular Economy in Transport Sector

Need s3–4

  • With growth, mobility systems face increased pressure → negative externalities without change of approach; transport is a key contributor to emissions and resource consumption; CE supports economic, environmental, social prosperity of city.
  • Mobility = key urban priority; central to how a city operates; affects quality of life, local environment, resource consumption; effective systems accommodate all modes; CE optimizes transport by integrating mobility planning with spatial planning while improving products & services.
  • Four application areas: vehicle design/manufacturing; transport infrastructure; mobility planning; transport-related policies.

CE in vehicle manufacturing s5–8

  • Enable repair, reuse, remanufacturing, recycling of components & materials; design vehicles to be easily repaired in service and materials recovered at end of use; energy used to create parts is not wasted if parts are reused.
  • Strategies: design for adaptable and shared use; maximum use potential & ability to remanufacture; modularity (parts swapped, space reconfigured) for longer use; flexible interiors in freight vehicles (simultaneous delivery & collection of goods).
  • Renault example: materials (copper, steel, textiles, plastics) from end-of-life vehicles (ELV) put into new vehicles with same performance as virgin materials; 36% of total mass of a newly produced Renault vehicle in Europe from recycled materials; 85% of an ELV is recyclable. [Diagram "vehicle of tomorrow": renewable energy source, electric & silent propulsion, autonomous, long battery life, second-life battery options, designed for disassembly, re-manufactured locally, durable/upgradeable/easily repairable, 3D printed parts, connected, shared.]

CE in transport infrastructure s9

  • Design infrastructure for adaptable use; construction materials with minimum negative impacts and reusable after life; dynamic design – ex.: dynamic street-use restrictions promoting specific modes at certain times (road space for bikes indicated by LED lights in road, freight-lane restrictions); simple low-cost interventions such as moveable street furniture.

CE in mobility planning s10–19

  • Compact city development for effective mobility; infrastructure promoting walking, bicycling; TOD is also part of CE in mobility planning.
  • London Walking Plan: by 2030 congestion projected to cost London GBP 9.3 billion a year; walking action plan aims at 1 million additional walking trips a day; invest GBP 2.2 billion (redesign streets, signposting & maps, more pedestrian crossings, better public transport); walkable London more land-use efficient, saves up to GBP 1.6 billion public healthcare costs, increases retail sales, reduces emissions, increases social cohesion.
  • Urban freight strategies: optimized freight movement; coordination between delivery/logistic companies → minimum resources, lower emissions per kg. Paris "logistics hotels" (inner-city green freight): logistics facilities back in city in high-density areas; reduce heavy vehicle use, emissions, noise; parcels pooled from suburban logistics centres via freight train or limited larger delivery shuttle-trucks; deliveries in smaller lower-emission vehicles e.g. electric tricycles carrying up to 180 kg; space rented by city at favorable rate but requires low-emission modes.
  • Resource flows (capital & material): plan mobility to minimize capital and resource use; a public transport system carries more people with less investment & fewer emissions.
  • China: CE opportunities could reduce total cost of access to mobility by CNY 12.6 trillion (USD 2 trillion) in 2030, an improvement of 36% over current path; decrease in non-renewable resource (incl. fossil fuels) consumption by 49% in 2030 and 71% in 2040.
  • São Paulo (reversing car-centric mobility): 2014 urban masterplan expands public & active transport; residents living near public transport from 25% in 2015 to 70% in 2025; 30% of urban development funds allocated; supports affordable housing and economic opportunities.

CE in policy & regulations s20–21

  • Policies, regulations, incentives promote CE; vehicle recycling/scrappage incentives, high manufacturing standards.
  • Vehicle Scrappage Policy of India: introduced in Union Budget 2021-22; formal regulations announced August 2021; ensures scientific scrapping; promotes material reuse; scrap value ≈ 4–6% of ex-showroom price of new vehicle; promotes rapid shift BS-IV → BS-VI; incentive such as no registration fee on purchase of new vehicle; keeps polluting old vehicles out.

Case study: Kera, Espoo, Finland s22–33 (Paper: "Creating a Circular City")

  • Partial case study; transport-related results used. Kera = new planned housing area in Espoo, Finland; typical example of former industrial area rezoned into housing with circular city targets; strategic target: international example of CE at city level. Study approach: alternatives → business models → regulations (realistic solutions for urban case district; examples of potential implementations; opportunities from regulatory perspective).
  • General data: Kera area 79 ha (Espoo 52,800 ha); inhabitants 13,300 (Espoo 289,731); jobs 10,000 (120,676); residential floor area 532,000 m²; annual heating demand 43 GWh, cooling 4 GWh, electricity 30 GWh; transport: 115,693 (person) trips; average trip length 12.60 km.
  • Objectives (9): carbon neutrality by 2030; achieving UN SDGs; emission-free public transport by 2030; enabling MaaS (Mobility as a Service); deployment of electric buses; completely renewable energy production by 2030; emission-free district heating by 2030; effective utilization of waste as material & energy; promoting utilization of surplus food.

Final conclusions s34

  • CE can be adopted in mobility planning, vehicle manufacturing, infrastructure development; approaches: TOD, EVs, walking & bicycling-friendly infrastructure; using waste as energy for vehicles and recycling vehicles.
Important Distinctions to Remember (L39)
  • CE in transport has 4 areas: vehicle design, infrastructure, mobility planning, policies. Mobility planning sub-themes: compact city/TOD, urban freight, resource flows.
  • Kera scenarios: 1 conservative (Finland 2035 figures) · 2 accelerated carbon-free tech · 3 ambitious CE (all hybrid/electric cars; PT carbon-free by 2030).
  • Recycling numbers: Renault 36% recycled mass, 85% ELV recyclable. Scrappage value 4–6%.
