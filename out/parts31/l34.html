ISO & ISO 14040 series s3–6

  • ISO: independent, non-governmental international organization; membership of 165 national standards bodies; develops voluntary, consensus-based, market-relevant International Standards; officially came into existence in 1947.
  • ISO 14040 series: LCA procedure; quantitative assessment methods for environmental aspects of a product/service over its entire life-cycle stages. ISO 14040 is the overarching standard encompassing all four phases of LCA; various standards supplement it.

Types of LCA s7–12

Three types: Conceptual, Simplified, Detailed – each with strengths/weaknesses; application depends on context.

TypeKey factsEV example (s12)
ConceptualSimplest; basic assessment from a limited, usually qualitative inventory; shows which components/materials have largest impacts and why; only helps decision makers identify which products have a competitive advantage; also called "Life cycle thinking" (UNEP-DTIE, 2003)Tail-pipe emissions for EVs are zero compared to substantial emissions by fossil-fuel vehicles
SimplifiedScreening assessment covering the whole life cycle; uses generic data and standard modules for energy production; focuses on most important aspects/stages; thorough assessment of reliability of results. Stages: Screening (identify parts of life cycle/elementary flows that are important or have data gaps) → Simplifying (focus further work on important parts) → Reliability (check simplifying doesn't significantly reduce reliability)General values of emissions from fuel-based vehicle manufacturing & operations compared with generic/secondary values of emissions from EV manufacturing, battery and electricity sources
DetailedFull process; extensive, in-depth data collection; focuses on target of LCA; if only generic data available, data must be collected specifically on product/service. Steps (Consoli et al. 1993): planning, screening, data collection & data treatment, evaluation, improvement assessmentExact data of all aspects of emission from mining of material up to disposal of battery or scrap calculated; assessment of benefits of EVs over regular vehicles

Case study: LCA of last-mile distribution logistics, Milan & Turin (Italy) s13–32

  • Study area: LTZ (Limited Traffic Zone) of Milan and Turin urban centres (Italy, Europe). Turin = important business & cultural centre; Milan = important historical city, second most populated in Italy after Rome. Area: Milan 8.2 km²; Turin 2.58 km².
  • LTZ: regulatory intervention to improve air quality, lower noise, make area pedestrian-friendly; generally in high-footfall urban centres; vehicle traffic allowed only in limited off-peak hours or few vehicle categories exempted. LTZs = important area of analysis of delivery activities by logistics operators.
  • Details: assesses environmental footprint of last-mile logistics by LCA; delivery activities in LTZ by a large private logistics operator; scenario analysis: current practice vs replacing diesel fleet with electric vehicles (EV).
  • LCA steps followed: functional unit & system boundaries → inventory of input/output items per life-cycle phase → assessment of potential impacts via specific indicators → interpretation of results (inventory analysis & impact estimation) relative to study objectives.
  • Goal – scenarios: Baseline = typical day of a single large private operator, exclusively diesel vehicles; ZEV (Zero Emission Vehicle) scenario = diesel vehicles replaced by EVs. (EV = electric vehicles; LTZ = Limited Traffic Zone.)

  • Data collected: types of delivery methods (emission class, max load capacity); number of vehicles per type; kg of packages delivered per vehicle per day; km travelled per vehicle per day. Observation period: Milan 03/07/2017–30/09/2017; Turin 21/04/2017–31/08/2017.
  • Ecoinvent = world's leading LCI database (transparency & consistency). Lorry classes (avg. load / GVW): 3.5–7.5 t: 0.98 / 4.98 t; 7.5–16 t: 3.29 / 9.29 t; >32 t: 15.96 / 29.996 t. Load factor = ratio of average load to total vehicle freight capacity. GVW = gross vehicle weight.
  • Diesel input data (s24): Lorry 3.5–7.5 t – max capacity 4.98 t, avg load 0.98 t, utilization 20%, fuel 0.1093 kg/tkm; Van type 1 – 1.25 t, 0.625 t, 50%, 0.2222; Van type 2 – 1 t, 0.5 t, 50%, 0.25. EV data (s25): Light – 730 kg, 24 kWh, 170 km; Heavy – 2000 kg, 28 kWh (1–3 batteries), 280 km (3 batteries).
  • Results (s26) change ZEV vs baseline: Milan – climate change −37%, PM +13%, photochemical ozone −47%, acidification −15%; Turin – climate change −49%, PM +4%, photochemical ozone −48%, acidification −24%. [Slide 26 table shows −49% for Turin climate change; slide 30 text states 50% – both as in source.] (s27–28 bar/box charts: phases = delivery operations, fuel production, road maintenance, vehicle maintenance, electricity production, road construction, vehicle construction; ZEV with current mix, 25/50/100% solar.)
  • Why Milan ≠ Turin results (s29): Turin fleet exclusively EURO 5 whereas Milan combination of EURO 4 & EURO 5; diversity of routes in the two LTZs; different EV composition – Turin also includes heavy ZEV vehicles with lower specific consumption per kg of goods.
  • Conclusions (s30–31): ZEV fleet cuts GHG by 37% in Milan, 50% in Turin; with current electricity mix particulate matter increases 13% in Milan, 4% in Turin; NMVOC cut by almost 50% in both. ZEV adoption should be combined with policies for a decarbonized power system. Sourcing 50% of electricity from dedicated photovoltaic columns makes both scenarios' emissions comparable; 100% solar → particulate emissions −7% (local vs regional emission perspective differs).

Conclusions s32

  • LCA should be done according to ISO standards; various LCAs per study requirement; LCA efficiently used for scenario comparison.
Important Distinctions to Remember (L34)
  • 14040 (overarching) vs 14041 (goal/scope + LCI) vs 14042 (LCIA) vs 14043 (interpretation) vs 14047 (LCIA examples) vs 14048 (data formatting) vs 14049 (examples for 14041).
  • Conceptual (qualitative, "life cycle thinking") vs Simplified (screening; generic data; screening→simplifying→reliability) vs Detailed (extensive data).
  • ZEV: GHG and NMVOC fall but particulate matter rises with current electricity mix.
